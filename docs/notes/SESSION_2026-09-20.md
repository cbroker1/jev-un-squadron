## The headline

Two things were being measured wrongly, and both were found today.

**Damage.** The project had scored damage by a hit-marker heuristic since the beginning.
It misses events: 6 of 33 across 20 runs. Health is byte 8 of the player's own record in
the object table we already export every frame, so **every run ever recorded already
contained the true answer**. It reads 8 at the start, falls by 1 or 2 at a hit, and reaches
0 exactly when a run ends dead. 212 runs were re-scored from data on disk.

The correction is not cosmetic. Three runs recorded as "one hit" had in fact lost all
eight health, because a single event at the far right of the deck takes everything at once:

```
R131   frame 20582  lost 1   the firing prelude
       frame 20766  lost 7   at (194,187)   dead
```

**No run has ever finished undamaged**, and the first damage lands at frame 20582, during
the fixed firing prelude, before Jev's first decision at 20663. Zero damage is currently
impossible from this setup regardless of policy. That is a separate experiment: a shorter
warmup.

## The Jev interface redesign

The vendor documentation is now saved locally in `docs/typesafe/` (109 pages), with
`docs/typesafe/README.md` listing the rules this project was breaking. The sharpest:
**Jev cannot do arithmetic or numeric comparison, and the documentation says to do it in
code** - while every option we sent carried about 1,600 characters of numbers for it to
weigh.

The Codex session built `brain/decisions.py`, policy `categorical-v1`: three atomic Choice
questions in one parallel call, with Python doing the eligibility, comparisons, objective
selection and fallbacks. Finishing its A/B and diagnosing the result:

| | request | latency | units | confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| legacy | 24.8 KB | 330 ms | 78% | median **0.27**, 17% above 0.5 |
| categorical, floor 0.5 | 4.1 KB | 253 ms | 71% | median **0.85** on movement, 0.90 on attack |

**The decomposition does what the documentation promises**: splitting one overloaded
question into three took median confidence from 0.27 to 0.85. Legacy had been acting on
0.27-confidence answers at every single decision.

The gameplay loss was not the decomposition but the fallback. All 276 overrides were low
confidence, none a measured constraint, so the policy was discarding its best answers for
"repeat the previous move, else hold". The floor turned out to be non-monotonic:

| floor | units | runs completed | fallbacks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.5 | 71% | 3/3 | ~92 |
| 0.0 | - | **2/5**, three died | 0 |
| 0.3 | 75% | 3/3 | ~33 |
| legacy | 78% | 3/3 | - |

Removing it entirely kills runs; 0.5 throws away good answers. The documentation's own
pattern is a **floor per decision, set by what being wrong costs**, which is now
implemented: 0.20 attack, 0.30 pickup, 0.45 positioning, 0.90 when escaping.

Latest three runs with that, plus the rest of Carl's answers: **75%, 75%, 81%**, damage 2,
3 and 1 - matching legacy at a sixth of the request size, and R141 took the least damage of
any run on the table.

## Carl's answers, now built in

- **Risk against health.** Accept risk for a power-up or an orange unit that drops one,
  because a stronger weapon makes the rest of the level easier; take fewer such risks as
  health runs down. The request now carries health, a posture, and whether a hit can be
  afforded. This is what mapping health unlocked.
- **Spacing.** 20 px, judged the same in every direction because fire comes from all of
  them, and 34 px beside a boss hull, because individual units are squishy and a boss takes
  time.
- **Default station.** Back third, mid height.
- **Fixed spawn script**, so a missed unit is always a positioning failure, never luck -
  not yet used; it allows an expected-unit manifest.
- **Power-up tiers stack**, which likely explains the measured cap of about six shots a
  second and the tanks that survive - not yet used.

## Earlier today

- **The boss.** Reached repeatedly and never killed; no part has ever been destroyed.
  Survival in the fight went 184 frames, to 269 with the quietest-altitude fact, to 382
  holding off the hull at the bottom left, best attempt 566. Carl's method - stay off the
  hull, hold the bottom left under the big straight missiles, shoot the small turning ones,
  go around the hull when it crowds - is in the request, and both missile types are
  distinguishable in the data exactly as he described: the big group turns in 4% of frames,
  the small ones in 92%.
- **A wall found once is remembered for the run**, keyed by level position, because shots
  stopping short is live evidence of structure the map does not have. Two stops make a
  wall; one can be an unclassified enemy.
- **The gun line is asymmetric and differs by target**, measured from the share of frames
  at each offset that were followed by a kill. Ground targets are only hit from level with
  them or below; the old symmetric band claimed hits from 8 px above, where the real rate
  is 5.8%. That was 40 decisions of a run shooting over a tank.
- **Going back for a tank is back and down.** The sustained-path fact judged a pure
  sideways hold and therefore said "this does not line up a shot" at every decision, even
  with the tank 0.2 px away.
- **VRAM terrain is a closed negative.** Best alignment over ~900 configurations scores
  92% against a null model that only knows "sky above, content below" at 93%. It needs the
  PPU's BG registers, which this core does not expose.
- **A gameplay regression check** (`benchmark.py`), because unit tests cannot see a
  regression in play, and a **run table** (`runs_table.py`) that records what each run was
  testing.

## The lesson that keeps repeating

**Facts help; instructions hurt.** Five instructions written by the agent each cost kills
and were reverted. Every measurement added helped. Every correction Carl gave - turrets
first, boss parts vulnerable, ground targets reachable with a small drop, hug the ground,
hold the bottom left - was right.

The second lesson, learned three times: **a signal that fires on nearly every option is the
same as no signal**, and one of those cost a run 9 kills.
